A new kind of social network

Not a feed. Your people, texting your assistant.

Your friends and family don't download anything or follow anyone. They text your assistant, and the group they're in decides what they can see and do. They see only what you allow, and you approve everything before it happens.

Groups decide who sees and does whatFamily, friends, your partner: each group gets its own commands. Friends can set up a coffee or a beer, or ask you for help with a move; other groups get commands when you turn them on. Nobody can read your calendar.
Private by defaultNo one sees your calendar, your address or anyone else in your life. Numbers you don't know get nothing automatic.

The Should way

GTD asks "what's the next action?" Should asks "who does it?"

Getting Things Done was built for one person keeping their own lists. Should is built for one person with a staff. Most things that come in never reach you: an agent handles them. You see only the steps that need your hands or your decision.

Capture → Clarify → Route → Organize → Review → Engage. Things come in on their own: calls, texts, mail, email. Agents work out what each one is and who should act on it. You check in with the morning text each day and a short sit-down each week.

Agent firstYour attention is the scarce resource. If an agent can do it, or do all of it but one step, it does.
One-way and two-way doorsEasy-to-undo calls, like wording, scheduling or a draft, agents just make and tell you in one line. Only hard-to-undo ones (money, sending, deleting for good, settings) come to you, once, named for what's at stake and with one recommendation.
Dates only for real deadlinesYour calendar holds what has to happen on a day: appointments, deadlines. Nothing else.
Agents do the waitingWaiting on a reply, a refund or a package is the agents' job, and you can see the list anytime.
Fewer, betterA short list you'll finish beats a long one you won't. Fewer things in flight at once.
Rest is part of the systemSundays are for rest. The staff plans around it.

A staff role, the Philosopher, looks after the Should way. It reads widely, keeps the ideas worth borrowing and proposes new ones. You decide which get tried.

The ground rules

They work for you, not the other way around

Nothing happens without your yesNothing gets bought, sent, booked or deleted until you approve that exact thing. They fill the cart; you check out.
One question at a timeEvery question comes with three options. No walls of text.
Quiet hoursNo texts between 9 PM and 8 AM, and nothing on Sundays.
No secrets storedNo passwords, no full account numbers. They can't sign in for you.
Everything is loggedEvery change has a name on it, so you can see who did what.
What it's built with

Off-the-shelf parts

No custom AI model and no startup. It's Claude, connected to the services you already use, running on a laptop at home. Some services are read through their official APIs, read only, with keys only you hold, instead of driving a browser.
